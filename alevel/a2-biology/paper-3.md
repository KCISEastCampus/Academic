## Entry diagnosis {#diagnosis}

**Self-written task — answer before opening the feedback.**

> A plant seedling grows in the light. State where the carbon in its new carbohydrate comes from. Explain why the seedling still needs respiration. A student says that sunlight is converted into carbon atoms. Correct the statement.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint: separate atoms from energy transfer</summary>

Track carbon dioxide into organic molecules. Then identify how energy is transferred to processes that need ATP.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Feedback and next step</summary>

Carbon atoms come from carbon dioxide fixed in photosynthesis; early growth can also redistribute carbon from seed reserves. Light supplies energy for photosynthesis, not new carbon atoms. Respiration transfers energy from organic substrates to ATP for processes such as active transport and biosynthesis. Plants respire in light as well as darkness. Revisit [photosynthesis](/alevel/a2-biology/photosynthesis/) if matter and energy became mixed, and [respiration](/alevel/a2-biology/respiration/) if ATP's role is missing.

</details>

## Transfer without a named method {#transfer}

**Self-written task.** Use complete English sentences and select the relevant processes yourself.

> In a closed, illuminated culture, an algal population stops increasing although light intensity is raised. Suggest two different limiting conditions. For one suggestion, explain a controlled investigation and a result that would support it. Explain why the original observation alone does not identify the cause.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint: choose a resource and a measurable response</summary>

Consider carbon dioxide or mineral supply. Change one proposed cause while keeping other relevant conditions comparable; use independent cultures.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>One defensible response</summary>

Carbon dioxide or a mineral nutrient such as nitrate could limit growth. Compare independently replicated cultures with and without added nitrate, keeping initial biomass, carbon dioxide, temperature and light conditions comparable. Measure population growth over the same interval. Greater growth after nitrate addition would support nitrate limitation under these conditions. No response to extra light only shows that additional light did not increase growth in that range; it cannot distinguish resource shortage from unsuitable temperature, waste accumulation or other causes. A population-growth measure also integrates cell division and death, not just instantaneous photosynthesis.

Retry: replace “population growth” with oxygen accumulation. Explain why your interpretation now needs to account for respiration as well as photosynthesis.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Check the retry about oxygen accumulation</summary>

Oxygen accumulation reflects oxygen production by photosynthesis minus oxygen consumption by respiration, with gas exchange and dissolution also relevant to the apparatus. More net oxygen does not by itself identify a change in gross photosynthesis; compare biomass and measurement conditions and account for respiration.

</details>
