## Diagnose an experimental claim {#diagnosis}

**Self-written task.**

> A student places ten seedlings in one dish with fertiliser and ten in one dish without fertiliser. After seven days, the mean seedling height is greater in the fertilised dish. The student claims that ten independent repeats prove that fertiliser increases growth. Evaluate the claim and improve the investigation.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint: identify what received the treatment independently</summary>

Which seedlings share the same environment? What else may differ between the two dishes?

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Feedback</summary>

The treatment is applied to each dish; seedlings within it share conditions. There is only one independently treated dish per condition, so dish effects cannot be separated from the fertiliser effect. Randomly allocate several independent dishes to each treatment, keep initial material and relevant conditions comparable, and compare suitable dish-level summaries. More seedlings in the same two dishes do not solve this problem. The observed mean difference supports a question to investigate, not proof of the general causal claim.

Retry: repeated readings of one plant's height are taken every hour. Explain why these readings improve the growth record but do not create new independent plants.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Check the repeated-height retry</summary>

The readings describe the same plant's trajectory and share its genotype, history and environment. They may be correlated over time. They provide more time points, but the number of independently sampled plants remains one; add independent plants or treatment units to estimate variation between them.

</details>

## Evidence of readiness {#readiness}

Attempt a method decision, a data task and a synoptic explanation with help closed. Check whether you can explain the *reason* for a control, choose the denominator and units, and connect entities with causal verbs. If you can only reproduce the worked example, change the organism, measurement or blocked step and retry. See [synoptic writing](/alevel/a2-biology/synoptic-writing/) for guided and independent tasks.
