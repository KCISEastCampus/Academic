## What enters a growing plant? {#start}

A plant's dry mass contains carbon atoms; light supplies energy, not those atoms. Photosynthesis couples energy transfer to the synthesis of organic substances from CO₂. After this lesson, trace the separate paths of carbon, electrons and protons, explain a limiting factor and interpret a measured net rate. Review chloroplasts, enzymes, ATP and diffusion in [prerequisites](/alevel/a2-biology/prerequisites/).

## Light-dependent reactions: two sides of a membrane {#light-reactions}

Chlorophyll and accessory pigments absorb particular wavelengths. Energy is transferred to a reaction centre, where chlorophyll electrons are excited to a higher energy level and leave for an electron acceptor. In the non-cyclic pathway, electrons lost from photosystem II are replaced by **photolysis of water**; oxygen is a by-product of splitting water, not of splitting CO₂.

$$2\mathrm{H_2O}\longrightarrow4\mathrm{H^+}+4e^-+\mathrm{O_2}.$$

Electrons pass along carriers in the thylakoid membrane. Their energy drives proton transfer **from stroma into the thylakoid lumen**; photolysis also adds protons to the lumen. Because the membrane limits free proton movement, a gradient forms. Protons flow **from lumen to stroma through ATP synthase**, driving phosphorylation of ADP. This is photophosphorylation.

Light excites the electrons again at photosystem I. They ultimately reduce NADP on the stroma side, with hydrogen, to form **reduced NADP**. ATP transfers energy; reduced NADP supplies reducing power to carbon fixation. Neither is the carbon source. Cyclic electron flow can supply extra ATP without net reduced NADP or oxygen production; the core model here follows non-cyclic transfer from water to NADP.

![Teaching schematic of a thylakoid membrane: light and water supply electron transfer; protons enter the lumen and return through ATP synthase to the stroma. ATP and reduced NADP support the Calvin cycle. Not to scale.](/assets/images/a2-biology/bl03-photosynthesis.svg)

## Calvin cycle: account for carbon and regenerate the acceptor {#calvin}

In the stroma, **rubisco** catalyses the reaction of CO₂ with five-carbon ribulose bisphosphate (RuBP). The unstable six-carbon product gives two molecules of three-carbon glycerate 3-phosphate (GP). ATP and reduced NADP from the light reactions enable GP to be reduced to triose phosphate (TP). NADP, ADP and phosphate return to the light reactions.

Most TP regenerates RuBP, using ATP, so further CO₂ can be accepted. Some TP leaves the cycle to form carbohydrates and other organic substances. Amino-acid synthesis also needs a nitrogen source; carbon fixation alone cannot supply nitrogen.

**Carbon-account demonstration — Self-written:** three CO₂ contain three carbon atoms. Three RuBP contain fifteen. They produce six GP and then six TP, containing eighteen carbons altogether. One TP (three carbons) is a net output; five TP (fifteen carbons) regenerate three RuBP. Two net TP can supply the carbon skeleton of one hexose. This bookkeeping does not mean the plant makes glucose in one single reaction.

<!-- task:P1 -->
**Self-written / 自拟题 — P1**

Complete the carbon account for six CO₂ entering the Calvin cycle: ___ RuBP react; ___ GP form; ___ TP are a net output; the remaining ___ TP regenerate RuBP. Explain why regeneration is essential.
<!-- /task:P1 -->

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint P1</summary>

Each RuBP has five carbons; each GP or TP has three. Use the three-CO₂ demonstration, then check all eighteen input carbons of that smaller account.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answer P1</summary>

6 RuBP; 12 GP; 2 TP net output; 10 TP regenerate RuBP. Without regeneration, the acceptor is depleted and further carbon fixation cannot continue even if CO₂ remains available.

</details>

“Light-independent” means that the Calvin reactions do not directly absorb light. They still depend on products of the light reactions and on enzyme regulation. After darkness begins, existing ATP and reduced NADP may support brief activity, but indefinite synthesis in darkness is not possible by this pathway. Initially, if GP reduction slows more than fixation, GP can accumulate; once RuBP becomes depleted, continued fixation also falls. Always specify the timescale.

## Limiting factors: test a proposed explanation {#limiting-factors}

A limiting factor restricts the rate under the **current** conditions. Raising that factor increases rate only until another process limits it. Low light can limit ATP/reduced NADP supply; low CO₂ can limit fixation; temperature affects enzyme-controlled reactions. Excessive temperature can disrupt enzymes and membranes, while stomatal closure during water stress can reduce CO₂ entry. A plateau alone does not identify which factor limits the rate.

**Worked comparison — Self-written; constructed data:** at constant temperature, raising light from 100 to 200 arbitrary units raises net oxygen output from 2 to 4 units, but raising it to 400 leaves output at 4. Raising CO₂ at 400 light units increases output to 7. This intervention supports CO₂ limitation at the original plateau; light saturation alone did not establish it. It does not show that CO₂ limits every point.

## RP7: measured oxygen is a net exchange {#rp7}

An illuminated plant both photosynthesises and respires. Net oxygen release equals photosynthetic oxygen production minus respiratory oxygen consumption. At the compensation point, these rates balance: no net gas exchange does not mean both processes stopped. If a dark respiration rate is added to a light net rate to estimate gross photosynthesis, state the assumption that respiration in light equals the measured dark rate.

$$P_{\mathrm{net}}=P_{\mathrm{gross}}-R,\qquad P_{\mathrm{gross}}=P_{\mathrm{net}}+R.$$

Use a measured light intensity where possible. Light intensity is approximately inversely proportional to the square of distance for a small source under suitable geometry, but ambient light and reflections can violate this relationship. Measure collected gas volume or use a calibrated oxygen sensor over a fixed interval; counting bubbles assumes similar bubble sizes and misses dissolved gas. Control water temperature with a bath, CO₂ availability, species, tissue amount, wavelength and acclimation time. Use independent plant samples and repeat each level; randomising order helps separate treatment from time effects.

Keep electrical lamps away from water, avoid lamp burns, and follow approved handling of glass and aquatic organisms. A planned method must still be tested for a usable range and stable readings.

## Pigments and chromatography: an RP3 bridge {#pigments}

Different pigments extend the wavelengths absorbed. An absorption spectrum describes light absorbed; an action spectrum describes a biological rate at different wavelengths under comparable illumination. Similarity supports a pigment's contribution, but the two graphs measure different things.

For chromatography, apply a small concentrated pigment spot to a pencil baseline above the solvent. Let solvent rise, mark its front immediately and measure from the same baseline to each spot centre and to the front. Pigments separate because their solubility in the mobile phase and attraction to the stationary phase differ. Compare Rf only under the same solvent and stationary-phase conditions; matching Rf supports, but does not uniquely establish, identity. Solvents may be flammable or harmful: use the school's ventilated setup, eye protection and no ignition source.

$$R_f=\frac{\text{distance moved by pigment}}{\text{distance moved by solvent front}}.$$

**Self-written / 自拟题 — P2**

A chloroplast preparation is supplied with constant light and then given much less CO₂. Predict the immediate effect on GP formation and the likely initial change in RuBP, assuming the regeneration reactions continue briefly. Explain why the supply of light alone cannot maintain sugar production.
<!-- /task:P2 -->

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint P2</summary>

Identify the substrate and acceptor in the rubisco reaction before thinking about ATP.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answer P2</summary>

Less CO₂ fixation reduces GP formation. RuBP can initially accumulate because it is regenerated but consumed more slowly. Light can supply ATP and reduced NADP, but these do not replace CO₂ as the carbon source. The prediction is an initial response; later regulation and substrate depletion can change pool sizes.

</details>

<!-- task:P3 -->
**Self-written / 自拟题 — P3**

An aquatic plant releases 6.0 cm³ O₂ h⁻¹ in light and consumes 1.5 cm³ O₂ h⁻¹ in darkness at the same temperature. Estimate gross oxygen production and state the required assumption. A second student moves a hot lamp closer and claims that the larger gas volume proves an effect of light intensity alone. Evaluate this claim and improve the design.
<!-- /task:P3 -->

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint P3</summary>

Respiration subtracts from the light measurement. The lamp changes more than one condition.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answer P3</summary>

Estimated gross production = 6.0 + 1.5 = 7.5 cm³ O₂ h⁻¹, assuming respiration has the same rate in light and darkness. The lamp may change temperature, affecting biological rates and gas volume. Use a thermostatic bath or suitable heat filter, monitor temperature and light intensity, control CO₂ and tissue amount, and replicate with independent samples.

</details>

<!-- task:P4 -->
**Self-written / 自拟题 — P4**

A pigment spot moves 36 mm while the solvent front moves 60 mm. Calculate Rf. A reference pigment has the same Rf in a different solvent. Explain why this does not establish that the pigments are identical.
<!-- /task:P4 -->

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint P4</summary>

The ratio has no unit. Consider what the solvent changes.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answer P4</summary>

Rf = 36/60 = 0.60. Solvent composition affects pigment solubility and movement, so values from different conditions are not directly comparable. Run a reference under the same conditions; even then, coincident spots can contain different substances.

</details>

<!-- task:P5 -->
**Self-written / 自拟题 — P5**

Retry: oxygen uptake and release are equal at one light intensity. Predict the sign of net oxygen exchange immediately after the light is switched off, assuming aerobic respiration continues. State why 'no net exchange' and 'no metabolism' are different claims.
<!-- /task:P5 -->

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint P5</summary>

Switching off light removes one contribution to the balance; it does not remove the plant's ATP demand.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answer P5</summary>

Net exchange becomes uptake: photosynthetic oxygen production falls while respiration continues consuming oxygen. At the original compensation point, equal opposing rates gave zero net change despite active metabolism.

</details>
