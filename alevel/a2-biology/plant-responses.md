## Start with relative growth {#start}

**Question:** how can the same signal cause roots and shoots to bend in opposite directions? Learn to connect stimulus, IAA distribution, the response of a particular organ and unequal elongation. Review cells, osmosis and experimental variables in [prerequisites](/alevel/a2-biology/prerequisites/).

<!-- task:BL04-P0 -->
**P0 · Self-written / 自拟题**

A flexible strip has two attached sides. Side A becomes longer than side B. Which side lies on the outside of the bend? Explain without using the word “auxin”.
<!-- /task:BL04-P0 -->

<details markdown="1">
<summary>P0 · Check your starting idea</summary>

A is on the outside: the longer side follows the longer arc. The organ bends towards the side that elongates less. This geometry will let you predict direction from growth, rather than memorise an arrow.

</details>

## IAA: a signal with conditions {#iaa}

Plant growth substances are signalling molecules active at low concentrations. They can act near their source or be transported to other tissues. A **tropism** is a directional growth response: positive means towards the stimulus, negative means away. It differs from movement of a whole organism.

Indoleacetic acid (**IAA**, an auxin) is produced in growing regions, including shoot tips, and transported to responsive cells. In an elongating shoot, an appropriate concentration promotes wall loosening; water uptake and turgor then allow the cell to extend. The wall must be able to yield: taking up water is not by itself an explanation of lasting growth.

Roots are more sensitive to IAA than shoots. Very low concentrations can promote root elongation; concentrations that stimulate a shoot may inhibit a root. At sufficiently high concentrations, shoot elongation can also be inhibited. Therefore “IAA promotes growth” needs an organ and a concentration range. A shoot-only experiment cannot supply a measured root curve.

### Worked mechanism and a missing link {#direction}

**Worked example · Self-written / 自拟题:** place a young shoot horizontally in darkness. Gravity leads to redistribution of IAA towards the lower side; IAA is transported, not simply a heavy liquid settling. At a growth-promoting concentration, lower-side cells elongate more than upper-side cells. The lower side becomes the outside of the curve and the shoot bends upwards: negative gravitropism.

With light from one side, IAA accumulates on the shaded side of a responsive shoot. Greater elongation there bends the shoot towards the light: positive phototropism. The stimulus differs, but unequal elongation is the link to curvature.

<!-- task:BL04-P1 -->
**P1 · Self-written / 自拟题**

A root is placed horizontally in darkness. More IAA reaches its lower side. At the concentrations present, IAA inhibits root cell elongation. Complete the chain: lower side has more IAA → ____ → ____ → direction of curvature.
<!-- /task:BL04-P1 -->

<details markdown="1">
<summary>P1 · Hint</summary>

Use the stated effect on root cells, then compare the two sides. The side that becomes longer must be on the outside of the bend.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>P1 · Explanation</summary>

More IAA on the lower side → less elongation there → upper side elongates more and becomes the outside of the curve → root bends down, towards gravity. This is positive gravitropism. The different direction is explained by the different response to IAA, not by IAA accumulating on the opposite side.

</details>

## Read concentration evidence carefully {#data}

<!-- task:BL04-P2 -->
**P2 · Adapted / 改编题**

Use the table and teaching graph. Explain the purpose of the zero-IAA treatment. Calculate the increase in mean elongation at 1.00 mg dm−3 relative to the zero treatment, as a percentage of the zero-treatment mean. Explain why the results do not establish direct proportionality. State one limitation of using one dish per concentration.
<!-- /task:BL04-P2 -->

<details markdown="1">
<summary>P2 · Hint</summary>

The denominator for a percentage increase is the baseline mean. For proportionality, examine whether elongation divided by concentration is constant. Identify which samples share a treatment environment.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>P2 · Answer and source boundary</summary>

The zero treatment shows growth without added IAA, allowing the added hormone's effect to be compared.

$$\text{increase relative to control}=\frac{5.4-1.4}{1.4}\times100\%=286\%\quad\text{(3 s.f.)}.$$

Mean elongation increases across these tested concentrations, but not in direct proportion: increasing concentration tenfold from 0.10 to 1.00 does not multiply elongation tenfold. A nonzero baseline is another reason not to draw a proportional relation through the origin. The MS accepts a broad “proportional” expression; this teaching explanation uses the stricter mathematical meaning.

Ten tips within one dish share treatment conditions. Dish effects are confounded with concentration. Use several independently prepared dishes per concentration, random allocation of comparable tips, and analysis that recognises tips nested within dishes. More measurements in the same dish do not remove that confounding.

</details>

## Independent investigation: test gravitropism {#investigation}

<!-- task:BL04-P3 -->
**P3 · Self-written / 自拟题**

Design an investigation to test whether gravity can cause curvature of a seedling shoot without directional light. Define the response measured, suitable controls and independent replication. Explain why measuring final length alone would not answer the question.
<!-- /task:BL04-P3 -->

<details markdown="1">
<summary>P3 · Hint</summary>

Separate direction from amount of growth. Specify the initial orientation, a way to remove directional light, and a common observation time.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>P3 · One defensible design</summary>

Use seedlings of the same species, similar age and size. Randomly allocate several independent containers to horizontal or upright orientation in darkness. Keep temperature, water availability, medium and duration alike; photograph briefly under conditions that do not introduce a sustained directional-light treatment. Measure the angle of the growing tip relative to its initial axis at the same times, using the same viewing plane and scale. Record individual values and compare container-level summaries.

Curvature of horizontal shoots in darkness supports a gravity response without directional light. Upright shoots help detect non-directional handling or growth effects; an additional one-sided-light treatment can examine light separately. Final length measures growth amount, not its direction. Avoid damaging tips and follow the laboratory's handling rules. This is preparation for RP10, not completion of the practical.

</details>

## Other plant signals {#other-signals}

### Ethene and fruit ripening {#ethene}

**Ethene** is a gaseous signal that promotes ripening in climacteric fruits such as bananas. Ripening involves coordinated changes including softening and changes in sugars and pigments. Controlling ethene exposure and storage conditions permits green fruit to be transported and ripened later. This does not mean every fruit responds identically.

### ABA, guard cells and stomatal closure {#aba}

During water stress, **abscisic acid (ABA)** signalling promotes loss of potassium and chloride ions from guard cells. Loss of solute raises their water potential (makes it less negative); water leaves by osmosis down the water-potential gradient, turgor falls and the stomatal pore narrows. This reduces water loss but also restricts CO₂ entry for [photosynthesis](/alevel/a2-biology/photosynthesis/). Hormone signalling is the information; ions and water are the materials moving.

<!-- task:BL04-P4 -->
**P4 · Self-written / 自拟题**

An inhibitor prevents potassium and chloride ions leaving guard cells after ABA treatment. Predict the effect on stomatal closure and explain the water-potential mechanism. Suggest one benefit and one cost of closing stomata during drought.
<!-- /task:BL04-P4 -->

<details markdown="1">
<summary>P4 · Hint</summary>

Trace solute → water potential → water movement → turgor. “ABA is present” alone does not guarantee that a blocked response can occur.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>P4 · Explanation</summary>

Ion retention limits the rise in guard-cell water potential, so less water leaves and turgor is better maintained. ABA-induced closure is reduced, assuming the inhibitor acts specifically as stated. Closing normally conserves water but restricts CO₂ diffusion and can lower carbon assimilation. Measure pore width and include solvent-only controls before attributing an experimental change to this particular mechanism.

</details>

## Diagnose and retry {#retry}

If you wrote “more IAA always means more growth”, revisit [organ and concentration](#iaa). If you chose the wrong direction despite the right elongation comparison, revisit P0. If you plotted zero on a log axis or called the trend proportional, revisit [the data](#data).

<!-- task:BL04-P5 -->
**P5 · Self-written / 自拟题 — retry**

A horizontal shoot is rotated through 180° about its long axis after a short period in darkness. Predict how the direction of NEW growth changes after IAA redistributes. Do not assume that already elongated cells become shorter.
<!-- /task:BL04-P5 -->

<details markdown="1">
<summary>P5 · Hint</summary>

Locate the new lower side. Distinguish the next period of growth from the curvature already produced.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>P5 · Explanation</summary>

After redistribution, the new lower side receives more IAA and elongates faster under the stated shoot conditions. New growth tends to curve upwards relative to gravity. Existing curvature need not disappear immediately, and past elongation is not reversed. Time is needed for redistribution and differential growth.

</details>
