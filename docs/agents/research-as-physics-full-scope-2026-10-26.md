5. **Specimens support assessment design, not a forecast of the next paper or complete coverage proof.** Their actual covers say Specimen 2018, V1.1, despite filenames containing 2016-v2. ### What the AS examinations actually assess

- **PH01 / Unit 1:** 3.1–3.3. **PH02 / Unit 2:** content-led questions on 3.4–3.5; Unit 1 physics principles can still be used. PH02 Section B explicitly draws on **3.1–3.5**, so errors, units, mechanics and energy cannot disappear after Unit 1.
- Each paper is **two hours, 80 marks**, worth **50% of AS / 20% of full A-level**. Section A: 50 marks, short and longer questions. Section B: 16 marks, data analysis/application. Section C: 14 marks, multiple choice.
- Full 9630 AS requires **all 3.1–3.5**, not only mechanics or the titles on a directory page. Photon physics, electron collisions and wave-particle duality are explicitly in 3.5 and thus PH02; atomic constituents and radioactive decay are in PH01's 3.3.
- AS assessment objectives are AO1 knowledge/understanding **28%**, AO2 application **36%**, AO3 information analysis/evaluation **28%**, AO4 procedure selection/description/evaluation **8%**. Do not substitute the three-AO framework or weightings of a different qualification. There are no separately allocated QWC marks, but unclear communication can lose the physics marks it fails to demonstrate.

## 6. Source defects and independent checks to carry into authoring

These are researcher's mathematical/physical checks, **not published OxfordAQA errata**. No board confirmation of a correction was obtained.

### Confirmed misleading graph statement in both specifications

Location: S44 §6.2.2 p. 40 / S51 §6.2.2 p. 41. The text associates negative gradient with indirect proportionality. A negative gradient alone shows a decreasing relationship; it does **not** establish inverse proportionality. For example, y = 2 − x decreases while xy is not constant. An inverse model y = k/x can be tested by constant product, appropriate ratio evidence, or linear plotting against 1/x with a suitable intercept/origin check.

Do not turn this source wording into a marking rule that every descending straight line is inverse proportion. PH01 Q11 itself needs the distinction between a negative straight-line gradient with intercept and a reciprocal-variable relation. [S44][S44]; [S51][S51]; [P1Q, Q11][P1Q]. S51's printed page was visually verified.

### Confirmed small-angle formula defect

Location: S44 §7 item 30 p. 42 / S51 §7 item 30 p. 43. Both files misassign the leading small-angle approximations for cosine and tangent. The mathematically correct leading forms, for small θ in **radians**, are:

- sin θ ≈ θ
- cos θ ≈ 1
- tan θ ≈ θ

At θ = 0.01 rad, cosine is about 0.99995 and tangent about 0.0100003, directly exposing the printed mismatch. Do not reproduce the erroneous forms. Small-angle reasoning is still listed in non-bold mathematical item 30; the defect does not justify deleting it or requiring exact equality. [S44, §7 item 30][S44]; [S51, §7 item 30][S51]. S51's formula and typography were visually verified.

### Confirmed PH01 MS Q07.2 substituted-force typo

Location: [P1Q Q07.1–07.2, p. 7][P1Q]; [P1M Q07.1–07.2, p. 6][P1M]. The preceding answer gives force 4.5 × 10⁵ N. The next substitution prints 4500, while its stated final energy is about 3040 J.

Independent check: extension = 45 × 3.0 × 10⁻⁴ = 0.0135 m. Therefore energy = ½ × 4.5 × 10⁵ × 0.0135 = 3037.5 J. Using the printed 4500 would instead give 30.375 J. The final energy is consistent with the preceding force; the substitution is not. The printed MS page was visually checked. If using this question, preserve official question identity and explain the correction without presenting it as board-issued errata.

### PH01 Q06.4 needs a force-model qualification

Location: [P1Q Q06.1–06.5, pp. 4–6][P1Q]; [P1M Q06, p. 5][P1M]. The MS uses momentum change divided by the 40 ms contact time, approximately 3.63 N, for the ball-on-lens force. Strictly, momentum change per time is the **net** force on the ball.

With upward positive, the average lens-on-ball contact force minus mg equals Δp/Δt. For the stated 0.016 kg ball, mg ≈ 0.157 N, giving contact-force magnitude about 3.79 N; Newton's third law gives the equal ball-on-lens magnitude. The MS value corresponds to neglecting weight during impact. Record the official answer and the approximation separately; never teach contact force = net force as a general identity.

### PH01 Q11's gradient sign should survive a magnitude shortcut

The supplied model gives gradient m = −h₀k. Thus extracting a positive h₀k means taking −m (or the gradient magnitude for this descending line). The MS's positive “gradient” value should not become a lesson rule that silently changes a negative gradient into a positive one. [P1Q Q11.3, p. 15][P1Q]; [P1M Q11.3–11.4, p. 10][P1M].
