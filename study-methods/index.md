This guide helps IGCSE and A-Level students turn previously studied material into knowledge they can explain and use. Bring your textbook, paper and an unanswered question. For unfamiliar concepts, begin with teaching or a worked explanation. This is study advice; your qualification determines syllabus coverage, required derivations and assessment criteria.

Start with your difficulty: [cannot work independently](#think-derive-practise) · [cannot understand yet](#when-stuck) · [keep forgetting](#review) · [need to learn from an error](#error-review).

## Think, reason and practise {#think-derive-practise}

The original contribution describes making a book “thicker” before making it “thinner”: first develop connections between concepts and methods, then condense connections you have checked into useful prompts. Short notes should lead back to reasons, conditions and examples, rather than leave only a list of formulas.

### Think: explain the concept {#think}

Choose a concept you are studying. Explain the question it answers, its terminology, symbols and units in your own words. For a model, identify the system, assumptions and conditions. Give an example and a situation where it does not apply. A gap tells you exactly where to return to the textbook.

In mathematics, check definitions, conditions and counterexamples. In science, connect phenomena, models and evidence. In economics, explain who responds to a change and through what mechanism. Evidence of understanding varies between subjects; not everything is a formula derivation.

### Reason: connect premises to conclusions {#derive}

Write the key steps and explain why each follows. Depending on the task, this may involve a mathematical derivation, a scientific mechanism or a conditional economic argument. When a step is missing, identify the definition, principle or evidence it needs.

Definitions, empirical laws and model assumptions have different roles. Not every relationship can be proved from one starting formula. Learn necessary facts and practise reconstructing required derivations independently; check your qualification's syllabus and marking materials for the expected depth.

### Practise: gradually remove support {#practise}

Read a complete worked example, noticing how conditions inform the choice of method. Close the solution and reconstruct key steps. Then attempt a question independently and try tasks that change the conditions, representation or direction of the question. Once foundations are established, mix previously learned topics so the question itself supplies the clues for choosing a method.

Practice can make basic steps fluent. Selecting models, checking conditions and evaluating evidence still require deliberate judgment. You do not need to discover every new principle unaided, and following a demonstration does not replace independent work.

### One revision session

**Invented learning scenario.** Lin follows an example and can repeat its calculation steps, but cannot explain the choice of method. A differently worded question leaves him stuck.

He returns to the concept and its conditions, explains which features justify the method, and closes the solution before writing that connection himself. He then attempts a question that changes what is being asked, explaining why the original method still applies or why another is needed.

He checks the reason for choosing the method separately from the accuracy of later steps. If he uses a hint, he records that support and later checks himself with another question. The purpose is to repair method selection, rather than simply accumulate similar exercises.

### Choose a next step {#try-next-step}

**Original task.** A student explains a concept and solves questions with the same form as a worked example. She cannot start a differently worded question that does not name the method. She plans to copy the original solution three more times.

Write what her current performance does and does not show, a task that would test the gap, and how you would provide help and check again if she became stuck. Write independently before opening the sections below.

<summary>Hint</summary>

Separate explaining concepts, following familiar steps and independently choosing a method. How could the task test selection without demanding knowledge she has not learned?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Suggested reasoning and another attempt</summary>

Her performance supports concept explanation and execution in a familiar form, but does not establish independent method selection. Give a new question within the material already taught, without naming the method. Ask her to identify conditions, propose candidate methods, justify a choice and then complete the solution.

If needed, revisit a specific prerequisite or give a hint about a condition. Record the help. Remove it and use another question to check selection again. Copying the old solution does not directly test this gap.

Try again: another student independently chooses a suitable method but repeatedly makes algebraic errors. How should the practice change? Check that your plan targets the specific manipulation and then returns to a complete question, rather than repeating method selection the student can already do.

## Understanding checklist: keep evidence {#understanding-check}

Choose a recently studied topic. Close your materials and complete these checks on paper. Mark each “independent / used help / not yet” and keep the actual work; no total score is needed.

1. **Explain:** state the central concept, symbols and necessary conditions in your own words.
2. **Connect:** write a key argument and justify its steps. Where derivation is inappropriate, identify a definition, empirical relationship or model assumption.
3. **Use:** solve a question whose answer you have not seen, explaining the choice of method.
4. **Check:** identify a possible failure condition or common error and explain how to detect it.
5. **Review:** after a delay, try a different question and record whether you work independently.

If you can repeat but cannot use a method, return to [removing support](#practise). For unclear symbols or conditions, revisit [the concept](#think); for a broken argument, revisit [the reasoning](#derive). Explaining to someone else can expose gaps, but check the explanation's accuracy and your independent performance.

## When understanding takes time {#when-stuck}

Understanding can develop gradually: first establish what a concept says, then use explanations to understand why, and practise applying it. Difficulty now does not imply permanent inability. It also does not justify skipping foundations needed for what follows.

Replace “I understand nothing” with a precise question, such as “Which condition justifies this step?” Check prerequisites, read a relevant explanation or complete example, and fill in one step yourself before trying again. Take the specific difficulty to a teacher or classmate if necessary. Rest when fatigued and return to check; a break does not guarantee an answer will appear.

## Review: retrieve, check and return later {#review}

Reading and highlighting can support initial organisation, but familiarity with a page does not establish mastery. After studying a short section, close it and write the main ideas, draw relationships or solve a question. Reopen it to check omissions and errors. This is retrieval practice.

Distribute later reviews across separate occasions. The timing of distributed practice depends on the material, forgetting and how long you want to retain it; there is no universal review calendar. Research supports retrieval and distributed practice, but does not guarantee a particular score improvement from this guide. See the [sources](#sources).

Make a small plan now: choose a topic, an unanswered question and a time to return. At that time, attempt the question independently before checking. If you recall nothing, narrow the task, relearn what is missing and schedule another attempt. If it is easy, consider a longer interval or a changed task next time.

## Error review: diagnose and check the repair {#error-review}

Copy this checklist onto paper and use it with a real question. “Careless” alone rarely identifies an actionable change: name the step and what you will do differently.

1. **First attempt:** record the question, original approach and help used.
2. **Specific break:** locate the first unsupported step. Is the problem conceptual, conditional, methodological, representational, linguistic or computational?
3. **Reason and repair:** explain the issue, locate the relevant explanation or example and practise what is missing.
4. **Retry with answers closed:** use a new question targeting the same difficulty and record any hints.
5. **Check later:** change the context or mix topics to test independent use again.

**Invented review example:** “Wrong method” is vague. “I chose by surface resemblance without checking the conditions” identifies a repair. Revisit the conditions, compare an applicable and an inapplicable example, then judge a new question independently. Use the subject content and the target task's requirements to judge correctness.

## Contribution and research sources {#sources}

Adapted primarily from pages 4–7 of 余道宽's 《学习方法最终版》, retaining the core ideas of thinking, reasoning, practising and gradually developing understanding. Website editors added the invented scenarios, original tasks and checklists, and clarified the limits of derivation, automaticity and revision advice. This is an adaptation, not a verbatim reproduction or an exam-board guide.

- Dunlosky et al. (2013), [Improving Students’ Learning With Effective Learning Techniques](https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266): a review of ten techniques, with stronger overall support for practice testing and distributed practice than for rereading and highlighting alone.
- Roediger and Karpicke (2006), [Test-Enhanced Learning](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x): in prose-learning experiments, retrieval benefits appeared on delayed tests; immediate performance and long-term retention should be distinguished.
- Cepeda et al. (2008), [Spacing Effects in Learning](https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/psy_facpub/1766/): suitable review gaps varied with the delay before the final test, rather than following one timetable for all material.

This guide has not yet been trialled with this website's students. Its learning effects require actual student work and later review to assess.
